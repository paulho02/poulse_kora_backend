## What went wrong with feeds

Most feeds today are sorted by a model trained on one number: how long you stay. It is very good
at its job. It learns that outrage holds attention better than nuance, that the tenth video is
easier to watch than the first post from a friend, and that a feed with no end is worth more than
one you can finish.

None of this takes bad intentions. It follows from what gets measured. A post spreads because the
ranking predicted you would react to it, and the people posting learn to write for the ranking
instead of for readers.

Reach, meanwhile, can be bought or gamed: paid promotion, twenty posts a day, bought followers, a
network of second accounts.

## The bet

Peerkola removes the ranking model and puts people in its place. A new post goes to a few readers
in its channel, and each of them decides. Forward it, and it goes on to a few more. Drop it, and it
stops there. A post people find worth passing on travels far. A post nobody wants to carry ends
quickly, no matter who wrote it.

That makes every reader an editor with one small vote. A single vote doesn't count for much.
Together they decide what spreads, and every step along the way is a person's decision.

## Give attention before you ask for it

If reach costs nothing, the loudest win. So posting costs tokens, and you earn tokens by reviewing
other people's posts. By the time you ask anyone for their attention, you have given some of yours.

The price goes up when a lot of people are posting at once and down when it's quiet, so nobody can
flood a channel. Money plays no part in it: you can't buy tokens, and nobody can pay their way into
your feed.

## Careful readers carry further

A forward from someone who actually reads is worth more than one from someone tapping through.
Peerkola looks at how carefully people read, mainly through occasional attention checks, and lets
careful readers' forwards reach a few more people. It never changes the reach of your own posts.
You paid for that reach with tokens.

Only the last 30 days count. Nothing sticks to you for good; a bad week is eventually forgotten.

## A feed that ends

Your feed holds a limited number of posts. Once you've decided on all of them it's empty, and
that's fine. It means everything that was meant to reach you has. New posts arrive when people
forward them, not because something needs to keep you busy.

## What we won't do

- Sort your feed by what you're likely to react to.
- Sell reach. No sponsored posts, no paid boosts, no tokens for money.
- Hide how it works. The rules described on this site are the rules the app follows.
- Measure success in time spent. Five minutes can be a good visit.

## What we don't know yet

Peerkola is an experiment, and some of its questions are still open.

- Whether the judgement of a few readers at a time finds good posts as reliably as we hope. Small
  channels are the hard case.
- What moderation looks like without a central feed. Forwarding slows down what nobody wants to
  carry, but that isn't a moderation policy.
- How prices should feel. Too cheap and channels fill with noise; too expensive and people stop
  posting.
- How the project pays for itself without breaking any of the rules above. We'd rather be paid for
  by the people who use Peerkola than by anyone who wants to reach them.

The beta is how we find out. If you have thoughts on any of this, [write to us](/#contact).
